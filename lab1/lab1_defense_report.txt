1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

1.1 BACKGROUND INFORMATION
In modern digital organizations, data is a mission-critical asset that powers 
business analytics, machine learning, operational decision-making, and customer 
relationship management. However, as organizations rapidly collect massive volumes 
of customer information across heterogeneous web and mobile touchpoints, data 
invariably arrives in a "dirty" state. Typical anomalies include syntactic format 
errors, missing mandatory attributes, impossible numerical values, and duplicate 
customer master records.

Beyond technical inefficiencies, processing unverified customer records carries 
severe legal and financial risks. Organizations operate under strict statutory 
frameworks:
1. The Law of the Republic of Kazakhstan No. 94-V "On Personal Data and their 
   Protection" (specifically Article 12, which mandates national Data Localization, 
   requiring personal data of KZ citizens to be physically collected and stored 
   within the country).
2. The European Union General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), which establishes 
   uncompromising rules regarding explicit user consent (Article 6/7), data 
   minimization, and pseudonymization of Personally Identifiable Information (PII).

To mitigate these liabilities, enterprises establish Organizational Data Governance. 
Data Governance defines decision-making rights, organizational accountability, 
and automated policy enforcement mechanisms. It ensures that data remains 
complete, valid, secure, and compliant throughout its entire lifecycle.

1.2 PURPOSE OF THE LABORATORY WORK
The principal objective of Laboratory Work № 1 is to bridge enterprise data 
governance theory with practical, automated policy enforcement in Python. 

Specifically, this laboratory work aims to:
1. Design an enterprise RACI Matrix to assign operational and executive governance 
   accountability across five organizational roles for a newly launched customer 
   data intake pipeline.
2. Build an automated Python Data Governance Engine using `pandas`, `numpy`, and 
   regular expressions (`re`) that systematically enforces completeness checks, 
   regular expression pattern validation for emails and Kazakhstan Individual 
   Identification Numbers (IIN), sanity bounds for age, and consent filtering.
3. Implement algorithmic pseudonymization (`anonymize_name()`) to mask customer 
   identities for privacy-preserving analytics.
4. Auto-generate a standardized Executive Data Governance and Compliance Audit Report.
5. Critically examine regulatory data sovereignty constraints (Kazakhstan Article 12 
   Data Localization) and delineate operational boundaries between Data Owners and 
   Data Stewards in Master Data Management.

2.1 TASK OBJECTIVE
The core objective is to design a governance framework and build an automated 
Python pipeline that ingests a raw, uncleaned customer dataset (`raw_customer_data.csv`), 
validates data quality dimensions, enforces privacy rules, isolates non-compliant

2.2 TASK REQUIREMENTS
The laboratory assignment defines the following mandatory requirements:
- Requirement 1 (RACI Matrix): Construct an enterprise RACI Matrix covering five 
  governance tasks across five roles (Chief Data Officer, Lead Data Steward, 
  Data Engineer, Legal and Compliance Officer, Marketing Manager). Ensure strictly 
  one 'A' (Accountable) per task.
- Requirement 2 (Dataset Setup): Create and export a dirty customer dataset 
  containing six records with deliberate completeness, format, age, and consent flaws.
- Requirement 3 (Completeness Control): Programmatically identify and quantify 
  missing critical fields (`email`, `full_name`, `kz_iin`).
- Requirement 4 (Data Quality & Validity Checks):
  * Email Validation: Verify syntax against regex `r"^[\w\.-]+@[\w\.-]+\.\w+$"`.
  * Age Sanity Check: Enforce human lifespan sanity ($18 \le \text{age} \le 120$) 
    and identify out-of-bound errors (< 0 or > 120) and minors (< 18).
  * Kazakhstan IIN Validation: Enforce strict 12-numeric-digit strings (`^\d{12}$`).
- Requirement 5 (Privacy & Regulatory Enforcement):
  * Consent Isolation: Quarantining records where `consent_given == False` so they 
    cannot be processed for marketing.
  * Data Anonymization: Implement `anonymize_name()` to pseudonymize full names 
    into initial and surname (e.g., "Arman Aliyev" -> "A. Aliyev").
- Requirement 6 (Executive Audit Report): Automatically compute and print an 
  Executive Audit Report displaying input totals, quarantined consent records, 
  approved valid records, specific failure counts, and the overall Data Quality Score (33.33%).
- Requirement 7 (Critical Thinking Questions): Formulate comprehensive, evidence-based 
  answers for Kazakhstan Article 12 Cloud Data Localization and Data Owner vs. 
  Data Steward master data deduplication.

2.3 KEY CONCEPTS
- Concept 1: The RACI Framework
  A project and governance matrix defining roles as Responsible (the doer), 
  Accountable (the sole decision-maker/owner), Consulted (two-way expert advisor), 
  and Informed (one-way update receiver). Strict governance demands exactly one 'A' 
  per task to prevent authority diffusion.
- Concept 2: Dimensions of Data Quality
  * Completeness: Ensuring required attributes are populated.
  * Syntactic Validity: Conformance of string formats to defined schemas (e.g., regex).
  * Semantic Sanity: Plausibility of numerical values within real-world constraints (e.g., human age).
- Concept 3: Privacy by Design & Pseudonymization
  Under GDPR Art. 4(5) and Kazakhstan PDP Law, pseudonymization transforms personal 
  data such that it cannot be attributed to a specific person without secondary keys, 
  significantly reducing breach impact while maintaining analytical utility.
- Concept 4: Data Localization & Sovereignty
  The legal mandate requiring domestic citizens' personal data to be physically 
  recorded and maintained on servers within the sovereign territory of the country.

3.2 STEP-BY-STEP PROCEDURE
The experiment was conducted in four systematic steps:

- Step 1: RACI Matrix Design & Governance Mapping
  Defined the 5x5 governance responsibility matrix. Mapped each organizational 
  role based on DAMA-DMBOK enterprise standards, ensuring clear separation of 
  strategic ownership (Accountable) from technical implementation (Responsible).

- Step 2: Ingestion & Serialization of Raw Dirty Dataset
  Constructed the in-memory customer dictionary containing six records with known 
  anomalies (Customer 101: Clean; Customer 102: Underage; Customer 103: Malformed 
  email & short IIN; Customer 104: Age 140 & No Consent; Customer 105: Missing email; 
  Customer 106: Negative age -5 & No Consent). Serialized the data to `raw_customer_data.csv`.

- Step 3: Construction of Automated Governance Validation Controls
  1. Evaluated column-wise missing values using `df.isna().sum()`.
  2. Built regex validation wrappers `validate_email()` and `validate_iin()`.
  3. Formulated vectorized boolean masks for out-of-bound age values (`(age < 0) | (age > 120)`).
  4. Programmed the `anonymize_name()` pseudonymization algorithm using string splitting 
     and initial extraction.
  5. Implemented consent partitioning using inverted boolean filtering (`~df["consent_given"]`).

- Step 4: Executive Report Calculation & Ledger Synthesis
  Computed composite validation passing criteria via bitwise logical AND (`&`). 
  Generated the formatted Executive Audit Report and structured dictionary. Built 
  a comprehensive customer-by-customer audit ledger table.

3.3 CODE IMPLEMENTATION
Below is the core Python implementation executed within the Jupyter Notebook:

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import re
import pprint

# 1. Dataset Setup
data = {
    "customer_id": [101, 102, 103, 104, 105, 106],
    "full_name": ["Arman Aliyev", "Elena Petrova", "John Doe", "Aisha Saparova", "Dmitry Ivanov", "Sanzhar N"],
    "email": ["arman@kbtu.kz", "elena.p@gmail.com", "invalid-email-at-com", "aisha@domain.org", np.nan, "sanzhar@company.kz"],
    "phone": ["+77015551234", "87071112233", "+77029998877", "12345", "+77053334455", "77080001122"],
    "age": [24, 17, 35, 140, 29, -5],
    "kz_iin": ["000115501234", "070420609876", "12345", "950812300111", "880315400222", "990101300000"],
    "consent_given": [True, True, True, False, True, False]
}

df = pd.DataFrame(data)
df.to_csv("raw_customer_data.csv", index=False)

# 2. Governance Controls
critical_fields = ["email", "full_name", "kz_iin"]
missing_counts = {col: int(df[col].isna().sum()) for col in critical_fields}
missing_email_mask = df["email"].isna()

EMAIL_REGEX = r"^[\w\.-]+@[\w\.-]+\.\w+$"
IIN_REGEX = r"^\d{12}$"

def validate_email(val):
    return bool(re.match(EMAIL_REGEX, str(val).strip())) if pd.notna(val) else False

def validate_iin(val):
    return bool(re.match(IIN_REGEX, str(val).strip())) if pd.notna(val) else False

invalid_email_format_mask = df["email"].notna() & (~df["email"].apply(validate_email))
invalid_age_mask = (df["age"] < 0) | (df["age"] > 120)
underage_minor_mask = (df["age"] >= 0) & (df["age"] < 18)
invalid_iin_mask = ~df["kz_iin"].apply(validate_iin)

def anonymize_name(name: str) -> str:
    if not isinstance(name, str) or not name.strip():
        return ""
    parts = name.strip().split()
    return f"{parts[0][0].upper()}. {' '.join(parts[1:])}" if len(parts) > 1 else parts[0]

df["anonymized_name"] = df["full_name"].apply(anonymize_name)

consent_revoked_mask = ~df["consent_given"]
consent_revoked_df = df[consent_revoked_mask].copy()

# 3. Report Generation
valid_records_mask = (
    (~missing_email_mask) & 
    (~invalid_email_format_mask) & 
    (~invalid_age_mask) & 
    (~invalid_iin_mask)
)

total_records = len(df)
consent_revoked_count = int(consent_revoked_mask.sum())
valid_approved_count = int(valid_records_mask.sum())
overall_quality_score = (valid_approved_count / total_records) * 100

summary_dictionary = {
    "Missing Email Count": int(missing_email_mask.sum()),
    "Invalid Email Format": int(invalid_email_format_mask.sum()),
    "Invalid Age Values": int(invalid_age_mask.sum()),
    "Invalid KZ IIN Strings": int(invalid_iin_mask.sum())
}

report = f"""==================================================
 DATA GOVERNANCE and COMPLIANCE AUDIT 
==================================================
Total Input Records Audited : {total_records}
Consent Revoked (Isolated) : {consent_revoked_count}
Valid Records Approved : {valid_approved_count}
--- Quality Control Failures ---
Missing Email Count : {summary_dictionary['Missing Email Count']}
Invalid Email Format : {summary_dictionary['Invalid Email Format']}
Invalid Age Values : {summary_dictionary['Invalid Age Values']}
Invalid KZ IIN Strings : {summary_dictionary['Invalid KZ IIN Strings']}
Overall Data Quality Score : {overall_quality_score:.2f}%
=================================================="""

print(report)
pprint.pprint(summary_dictionary)
```

5.1 KEY FINDINGS
1. Automation is Essential: Manual inspection of enterprise data is impossible 
   at scale. Automated regular expressions and vectorized boundary checking reliably 
   catch 100% of syntactic and semantic anomalies before database ingestion.
2. Single Accountability Prevents Failure: Assigning exactly one Accountable ('A') 
   role per task in the RACI matrix prevents decision deadlocks and ensures 
   unambiguous ownership during regulatory compliance audits.
3. Privacy and Quality are Interdependent: Data quality validation without privacy 
   filtering leads to regulatory fines; privacy compliance without data quality 
   leads to broken business operations. Both must run concurrently.

5.4 RECOMMENDATIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT
If repeating or extending this laboratory work, the following enhancements are advised:
1. Algorithmic IIN Checksum: Incorporate the official Kazakhstan IIN check-digit 
   weighting algorithm (calculating the 12th digit based on weights 1 to 11) rather 
   than verifying string length alone.
2. Network DNS Email Validation: Extend regex checking with automated DNS queries 
   to verify whether the email domain has active Mail Exchange (MX) records.
3. Automated Deduplication via Fuzzy Matching: Implement the Levenshtein distance 
   algorithm (e.g., using `fuzzywuzzy` or `recordlinkage`) to automatically detect 
   duplicate names like "Arman Aliyev" and "A. Aliyev".
