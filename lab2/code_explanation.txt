1. THE BIG PICTURE: WHAT DID WE BUILD AND WHY?
================================================================================
In most poorly organized companies, employee and project information is initially 
dumped into a giant, messy Excel spreadsheet. 

For example, a single row might contain an employee's name, email, department, 
salary, and three different projects they are working on, all crammed into one cell.
This leads to chaos:
- If someone's salary changes, you have to remember to update 10 different rows. 
  If you forget one row, your database is corrupted (Update Anomaly).
- You cannot create a new department unless you hire someone into it first (Insertion Anomaly).
- If the only employee working on a project leaves the company, deleting their row 
  accidentally deletes the entire project from the company's records (Deletion Anomaly).

To solve this, we took the chaotic spreadsheet, applied Edgar F. Codd's mathematical 
normalization rules (1NF, 2NF, 3NF), designed a clean Entity-Relationship (ER) model 
in Mermaid.ai, wrote a rock-solid PostgreSQL schema with declarative constraints, 
and built a working Python microservice with 10 automated unit tests that prove 
our database cannot be corrupted.

3. THE 10 BUSINESS REQUIREMENTS IN PLAIN ENGLISH
================================================================================
Every database must mirror the real world. We formulated 10 explicit rules:
- BR-01 (Departments): Departments have unique names and positive operating budgets.
- BR-02 (Managers): Each department has exactly one manager who is an active employee.
- BR-03 (Employees): Employees have a unique 12-digit IIN, corporate email, minimum wage 
  compliance (>= 85,000 KZT), and must be at least 18 years old at hire.
- BR-04 (Salary Grades): Job roles define [min_salary, max_salary]. An employee's base salary 
  must fall within their job role's grade.
- BR-05 (Projects): Projects have unique names, positive budgets, and valid date sequencing (end_date >= start_date).
- BR-06 (Staffing M:N): An employee can work on multiple projects; a project has multiple staff. 
  Tracked via `project_assignments` with weekly hours between 1 and 40, and no duplicate assignments.
- BR-07 (Leave Requests): Employees request leave (Vacation, Sick, Unpaid) with date ordering and manager approval.
- BR-08 (Performance Reviews): Quarterly reviews by supervisors scored 1 to 5 with feedback.
- BR-09 (Payroll Records): Monthly payroll ledger ensuring net pay = gross pay - deductions (net_pay <= gross_pay).
- BR-10 (Integrity Protection): Departments with employees cannot be dropped (ON DELETE RESTRICT). 
  Assignments cascade on employee termination, but financial records are preserved for auditing.

5. THE NORMALIZATION JOURNEY: UNF → 1NF → 2NF → 3NF
================================================================================

Why do we normalize?
E. F. Codd proved that unnormalized tables cause THREE FATAL ANOMALIES:
1. Insertion Anomaly: You want to insert a project or department, but because the table 
   requires an employee ID as part of the primary key, you cannot add the department 
   unless an employee is hired immediately!
2. Update Anomaly: Department "Software Engineering" appears in 50 rows. If the company 
   renames it to "AI & Engineering", you have to update 50 rows. If your script crashes 
   halfway, half the company is in one department and half in another.
3. Deletion Anomaly: If the only employee working on Project X resigns, deleting their row 
   permanently deletes all knowledge of Project X from the database!

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 THE UNNORMALIZED FORM (UNF) TABLE: WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Before normalization, companies typically store organizational data in a single, 
flat spreadsheet table where all entities (employees, departments, job roles, and 
projects) are merged into one monolithic row.

Schema:
UNF_HR_SPREADSHEET (emp_id, full_name, corporate_email, dept_id, dept_name, dept_manager, 
                    job_id, job_title, salary, {proj_id, proj_name, proj_role, weekly_hours})

+--------+-----------------+-----------------------+---------+----------------------+----------------+--------+--------------------+-----------+------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| emp_id | full_name       | corporate_email       | dept_id | dept_name            | dept_manager   | job_id | job_title          | salary    | Projects Assigned {proj_id, proj_name, proj_role, weekly_hours}        |
+--------+-----------------+-----------------------+---------+----------------------+----------------+--------+--------------------+-----------+------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 101    | Didar Auyesbay  | d.auyesbay@company.kz | 10      | Software Engineering | Didar Auyesbay | 1      | Lead Architect     | 1,250,000 | (501, HR Automation, Tech Lead, 20), (502, Core Banking, Consult, 15) |
| 102    | Aizhan Omarova  | a.omarova@company.kz  | 20      | Human Resources      | Aizhan Omarova | 3      | HR Director        |   850,000 | (501, HR Automation, Product Owner, 10)                                |
| 103    | Arman Kasymov   | a.kasymov@company.kz  | 10      | Software Engineering | Didar Auyesbay | 2      | Senior Dev         |   750,000 | (501, HR Automation, Backend Dev, 40)                                 |
| 104    | Dana Serikova   | d.serikova@company.kz | 30      | Finance              | Dana Serikova  | 5      | Financial Analyst  |   550,000 | (502, Core Banking, Auditor, 10)                                      |
+--------+-----------------+-----------------------+---------+----------------------+----------------+--------+--------------------+-----------+------------------------------------------------------------------------+

Why this table is Unnormalized (UNF):
1. Non-Atomic Attribute: "full_name" packs both first and last name into one cell.
2. Repeating Groups: "Projects Assigned" contains an array/list of multiple projects 
   crammed into a single cell (e.g., employee 101 has 2 projects in one cell).
3. Redundancy: Department details and job titles are repeatedly duplicated on every 
   employee row.
4. No Scalable Primary Key: You cannot uniquely identify individual project assignments 
   without breaking the nested list structure.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.2 MERMAID ER MODEL OF THE UNNORMALIZED TABLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In an unnormalized architecture, there are NO separated tables, NO relationships, 
and NO foreign keys. All attributes are forced into a single monolithic entity:

```mermaid
erDiagram
    UNNORMALIZED_HR_SPREADSHEET {
        int emp_id "Non-scalar / Unstable identifier"
        string full_name "NON-ATOMIC COMPOSITE: contains First + Last name"
        string corporate_email "Email address"
        int dept_id "Redundant department identifier"
        string dept_name "Redundant department name"
        string dept_manager "Redundant department manager name"
        int job_id "Redundant job identifier"
        string job_title "Redundant job position title"
        numeric salary "Base salary"
        string projects_assigned "REPEATING GROUP: nested array of (proj_id, proj_name, role, hours)"
    }
```

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 1: UNF to 1NF (First Normal Form)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- UNF Problem: In the unnormalized sheet:
  - "Full Name" contains both first and last names (non-atomic).
  - "Projects" is a repeating group (multiple projects comma-separated in one cell).
- 1NF Rule: All attributes must be ATOMIC (indivisible), and every row must be unique.
- Transformation:
  - Split `full_name` into `first_name` and `last_name`.
  - Flatten the repeating projects so each employee-project pairing gets its own row.
  - Establish a composite primary key: `(employee_id, project_id)`.
- Anomalies remaining in 1NF: Partial dependencies exist!

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 2: 1NF to 2NF (Second Normal Form)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- 2NF Rule: Must be in 1NF, AND no non-key attribute can be dependent on only a PART 
  of a composite primary key (No Partial Functional Dependencies).
- Analysis:
  - Our composite primary key is: `{employee_id, project_id}`.
  - Does `first_name`, `email`, or `salary` depend on `project_id`? NO! They depend 
    ONLY on `employee_id`. That is a partial dependency!
  - Does `project_name` depend on `employee_id`? NO! It depends ONLY on `project_id`.
  - Only `role_in_project` and `weekly_hours` depend on BOTH `employee_id` AND `project_id`!
- Transformation:
  - Decompose into 3 separate tables:
    1. `EMPLOYEE_2NF` (emp_id [PK], first_name, last_name, email, dept_id, dept_name, job_id, job_title, salary)
    2. `PROJECT_2NF` (proj_id [PK], proj_name)
    3. `PROJECT_ASSIGNMENT` (emp_id [FK], proj_id [FK], role_in_project, weekly_hours)
- Anomalies remaining in 2NF: Transitive dependencies exist inside `EMPLOYEE_2NF`!

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 3: 2NF to 3NF (Third Normal Form)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- 3NF Rule: Must be in 2NF, AND no non-key attribute can depend transitively on the 
  primary key via another non-key attribute (No Transitive Dependencies).
- Analysis inside `EMPLOYEE_2NF`:
  - `emp_id → dept_id`, AND `dept_id → dept_name, dept_manager`.
    Therefore: `emp_id → dept_name` is a transitive dependency!
  - `emp_id → job_id`, AND `job_id → job_title`.
    Therefore: `emp_id → job_title` is a transitive dependency!
- Transformation:
  - Extract the determinants into their own independent tables:
    1. `DEPARTMENTS` (department_id [PK], dept_name, location, budget, manager_id)
    2. `JOB_ROLES` (job_id [PK], job_title, min_salary, max_salary)
    3. `EMPLOYEES` (employee_id [PK], first_name, last_name, email, salary, department_id [FK], job_id [FK])
- RESULT:
  "Every non-key attribute depends on the key (1NF), the whole key (2NF), and nothing but the key (3NF)."
  All three anomalies (Insertion, Update, Deletion) are completely ELIMINATED!

6. POSTGRESQL DDL ARCHITECTURE: WHY EVERY LINE MATTERS
================================================================================
Take a look at `lab2/sql/schema_hr_3nf.sql`:

1. The Circular Foreign Key Trick:
   - A Department has a `manager_id` pointing to `employees`.
   - But an Employee has a `department_id` pointing to `departments`.
   - If you try to create both at the same time with strict NOT NULL foreign keys, 
     PostgreSQL throws an error because neither table exists yet!
   - Solution:
     Create `departments` first with `manager_id INT NULL`.
     Then create `employees` with `REFERENCES departments(department_id)`.
     Finally, run:
     `ALTER TABLE departments ADD CONSTRAINT fk_department_manager FOREIGN KEY (manager_id) REFERENCES employees(employee_id) ON DELETE SET NULL;`
     This allows departments to be seeded cleanly!

2. Check Constraints (CHECK):
   - `CHECK (budget > 0)`: Prevents negative or zero department budgets.
   - `CHECK (min_salary <= max_salary)`: Prevents inverted salary grades.
   - `CHECK (base_salary >= 85000.00)`: Enforces Kazakhstan statutory minimum wage!
   - `CHECK (birth_date <= hire_date - INTERVAL '18 years')`: Guarantees legal adult employment.
   - `CHECK (weekly_hours BETWEEN 1 AND 40)`: Enforces standard labor law working hours.
   - `CHECK (email ~* '^[A-Za-z0-9._%+-]+@[A-Za-z0-9.-]+\.[A-Za-z]{2,}$')`: Validates corporate email regex.
   - `CHECK (iin ~ '^[0-9]{12}$')`: Enforces exact 12-digit national ID.

3. Referential Actions:
   - `ON DELETE RESTRICT` on `departments`: If a manager tries to delete the Engineering 
     department while employees are assigned, PostgreSQL aborts and blocks the delete!
   - `ON DELETE CASCADE` on `project_assignments`: If an employee is terminated, their 
     temporary project assignments are automatically cleaned up, preventing orphan records.
   - `ON DELETE SET NULL` on `manager_id`: If a manager resigns, the department is not 
     deleted; its manager field is simply set to NULL until a replacement is appointed.

Q1: Why did you choose the Human Resources (HR) domain?
A1: "According to the student assignment rules, domain selection is strictly 
governed by the last digit of the student identification number. My Student ID is

Q2: What is the difference between 1NF, 2NF, and 3NF?
A2: "1NF eliminates repeating groups and enforces atomic values. 2NF is in 1NF and 
eliminates partial functional dependencies on composite primary keys. 3NF is in 2NF 
and eliminates transitive dependencies, meaning non-key attributes depend only directly 
on the primary key."

Q3: Can you explain an Insertion Anomaly that existed before 3NF?
A3: "In the unnormalized table where Employee and Project were merged into a composite 
key (emp_id, proj_id), we could not insert a newly created Department or Project without 
first hiring an employee to staff it, because primary key columns cannot contain NULL values. 
In 3NF, Departments and Projects are independent tables and can be created at any time."

Q4: How did you resolve the Many-to-Many (M:N) relationship between Employee and Project?
A4: "In relational theory, Many-to-Many relationships cannot be implemented directly 
with foreign keys without creating massive redundancy. We resolved it by creating an 
associative junction entity, `project_assignments`, which decomposes the M:N into two 
1:N relationships (`departments -> assignments` and `projects -> assignments`). This table 
also holds intersection attributes like `weekly_hours` and `role_in_project`, and enforces a 
composite UNIQUE constraint on `(employee_id, project_id)`."

Q5: How did you handle the circular foreign key dependency between Department and Employee?
A5: "A department references an employee as its manager, but an employee references a department 
as their employer. In PostgreSQL, this circular dependency was solved by creating the departments 
table with a nullable `manager_id`, creating the employees table, and then adding the foreign key 
constraint via `ALTER TABLE ... ON DELETE SET NULL`. In SQLAlchemy, we used `use_alter=True` and 
`post_update=True`."

Q6: What happens if someone tries to insert an employee with a salary below 85,000 KZT?
A6: "The PostgreSQL engine immediately rejects the transaction with a CHECK constraint 
violation (`chk_minimum_wage_statutory`), and our Python REST API returns HTTP status code 
422 Unprocessable Entity. This guarantees statutory legal compliance."

Q7: What is the difference between ON DELETE RESTRICT and ON DELETE CASCADE in your schema?
A7: "We use ON DELETE RESTRICT on departments and job roles so that a manager cannot accidentally 
delete a department while active employees belong to it. Conversely, we use ON DELETE CASCADE 
on project assignments so that when a temporary worker leaves, their assignment records are 
automatically cleaned up to prevent orphaned rows."

Q8: What candidate keys exist in your Employee table?
A8: "In our Employee table, the primary key is the surrogate `employee_id`. The natural 
candidate keys are `iin` (the 12-digit national identity number) and `email` (the corporate email 
address). Both are enforced with UNIQUE constraints."

Q9: What is the purpose of the analytical reporting view `v_department_salary_expenditure`?
A9: "It joins departments and employees, aggregates total headcount and monthly base salary spend, 
and computes annual budget utilization as a percentage. This enables real-time executive financial 
auditing without having to write complex multi-table joins repeatedly."

Q10: Why did you write automated tests in Pytest?
A10: "Automated tests prove that our theoretical business requirements (BR-01 through BR-10) are 
strictly enforced in code. We wrote 10 test cases testing valid and invalid inputs (such as duplicate 
emails, negative budgets, and hours over 40). All 10 tests passed with 100% success in 0.32 seconds."
